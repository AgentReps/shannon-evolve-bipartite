# Problem: distributed bipartite matching. You evolve two purely-local rules in
# solution.py — thin(degree, rng) at NOTIFY and select(neighbor_degrees, rng) at
# GRANT — to maximize the mean matching fraction over the fixed density sweep
# (N=144, Binomial, d in {2,3,4,5,6,8,10}). Respect the locality contract: the
# rules only ever see one sender's own out-degree / own neighbors' degrees.
# Baseline DB(0) ~0.63; the bar to beat is 2CGS ~0.73 (robust, tuning-free).
#
# Pick families that are genuinely different — variants of the same idea
# converge into the same local optimum. A good bias names a *rule family* and a
# *parameter to move*, not just "do better".

selection tuning: DB(alpha) with alpha <= 0 — sweep the exponent from uniform (0) toward greedy (-inf) and find the degree-bias that maximizes the mean fraction across the sweep; remember the optimum grows with density (greedy when sparse, alpha ~ -1.3 when dense)
thinning: cap the notified out-degree with max(k) and dilute with Bern(q) — find the k (2CGS's sweet spot is k=2) and q that reshape the intention-graph degrees for the most matches, paired with greedy selection
joint thinning x selection: co-tune the thinning cap K and the selection exponent ALPHA together; the goal is to beat 2CGS's ~0.73 robustly across ALL densities, not just one
novel local rules: invent leak-free decision functions beyond plain DB(alpha) — degree-threshold grants, degree-adaptive thinning (keep more neighbors when your own degree is high), smarter randomized-greedy tie handling, or two-stage local heuristics
